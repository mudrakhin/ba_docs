## Module 1: The Fundamentals of Agile

### What is Agile?

Agile is not a single method but a **project management philosophy** that prioritizes flexibility, customer collaboration, and delivering value in small, frequent increments. It's a mindset shift away from rigid, upfront planning.

### Agile vs. Traditional (Waterfall)

Agile contrasts sharply with traditional models, which follow a linear and sequential path.

| Aspect | Agile | Waterfall |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approach | Iterative & Incremental | Linear & Sequential |
| Flexibility | Welcomes change | Resists change |
| Delivery | Small, frequent releases | One single release |

### The 4 Values of the Agile Manifesto

### The 12 Principles of Agile

The 12 Principles expand on the values, guiding teams on how to implement the Agile mindset in practice.

1.  Our highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.
2.  Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.
3.  Deliver working software frequently, from a couple of weeks to a couple of months.
4.  Business people and developers must work together daily throughout the project.
5.  Build projects around motivated individuals. Give them the environment and support they need.
6.  The most efficient and effective method of conveying information is face-to-face conversation.
7.  Working software is the primary measure of progress.
8.  Agile processes promote sustainable development. The sponsors, developers, and users should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely.
9.  Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility.
10.  Simplicity—the art of maximizing the amount of work not done—is essential.
11.  The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams.
12.  At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly.

## Module 2: Scrum 101 - A Deep Dive

### What is Scrum?

Scrum is the most popular **framework** for implementing Agile. It's a lightweight, iterative approach that helps teams manage complex projects by providing a clear structure of roles, events, and artifacts.

#### The 3 Pillars of Empiricism

🔍

Transparency

Key aspects are visible to all.

🧐

Inspection

Regularly check progress.

🔄

Adaptation

Adjust to minimize deviation.

### The 5 Scrum Values

These values are the foundation upon which the pillars and the entire framework are built.

### The 3 Scrum Roles (Accountabilities)

#### The Product Owner (PO)

The **"What" & "Why"**. Accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team. Manages the Product Backlog.

#### The Scrum Master (SM)

The **"How" (Process)**. A servant-leader accountable for establishing Scrum as defined. Helps the team understand Scrum theory and practice, and removes impediments.

#### The Developers

The **"How" (Execution)**. A self-organizing, cross-functional group of professionals who do the work of creating a usable Increment each Sprint.

### The 3 Scrum Artifacts (Work & Value)

#### Product Backlog

An emergent, ordered list of **everything that is known to be needed** in the product. It is the single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team.

#### Sprint Backlog

The **plan for the Sprint**. It's composed of the Sprint Goal, the set of Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint, and a plan for delivering them.

#### Increment

A concrete **stepping stone toward the Product Goal**. It is the sum of all Product Backlog items completed during a Sprint, and must be usable.

## Module 3: Implementing Scrum - The 5 Events

Scrum is implemented through a series of 5 events that create a regular cadence, or "heartbeat," for the project. This cycle is called **The Sprint**.

### The Scrum Process Flow

#### Product Backlog

The master list of all work

#### 1\. Sprint Planning

Define Sprint Goal & select work

#### Sprint Backlog

The plan for this Sprint

#### 2\. The Sprint (1-4 Weeks)

The container for all other events. Work is done here.

#### 3\. Daily Scrum (15 min)

Daily inspection and adaptation of the plan

#### "Done" Increment

The usable, completed work

#### 4\. Sprint Review

Inspect the Increment with stakeholders. Adapt Product Backlog.

#### 5\. Sprint Retrospective

Inspect the process. Plan improvements for next Sprint.

⟲

Cycle Repeats

### Sprint Timeboxing (Based on 4-Week Sprint)

The vast majority of time is dedicated to development work, with events providing structure and feedback loops.

### Event Details

*   **1\. The Sprint:** The "heartbeat" of Scrum, a fixed-length event of one month or less which contains all other events.
*   **2\. Sprint Planning:** Lays out the work to be performed. Timeboxed to a maximum of 8 hours for a one-month Sprint.
*   **3\. Daily Scrum:** A 15-minute event for the Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog.
*   **4\. Sprint Review:** Inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations. Timeboxed to a maximum of 4 hours for a one-month Sprint.
*   **5\. Sprint Retrospective:** Plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness. Timeboxed to a maximum of 3 hours for a one-month Sprint.

## Module 4: Applying Agile in the Organization

While Scrum is designed for a single team, larger organizations often need frameworks to **scale Agile** across multiple teams, departments, and portfolios.

### Scaling Agile: SAFe

The **Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe)** is one of the most common frameworks for applying Agile principles at an enterprise scale.

It provides a structured approach for large organizations to coordinate multiple Agile teams (called "Agile Release Trains") working on a single solution, manage portfolios of projects, and align development with strategic business goals.

### The 4 Levels of SAFe

#### Full SAFe

The most comprehensive level, integrating all others.

#### Portfolio SAFe

Provides strategic guidance and investment funding.

#### Large Solution SAFe

For complex solutions requiring multiple "Agile Release Trains".

#### Essential SAFe

The most basic configuration and starting point.

Adopting Agile and Scrum isn't just a process change; it's an investment in efficiency, quality, and people. This is reflected in the high demand and compensation for skilled Agile professionals.
