## Module 1: The Fundamentals of Agile

### What is Agile?

Agile is not a single method but a project management philosophy that prioritizes flexibility, customer collaboration, and iterative delivery.

### Agile vs. Traditional (Waterfall)

| Aspect      | Agile                    | Waterfall              |
|-------------|--------------------------|------------------------|
| Approach    | Iterative & Incremental  | Linear & Sequential    |
| Flexibility | Welcomes change          | Resists change         |
| Delivery    | Small, frequent releases | One single release     |

### The 4 Values of the Agile Manifesto

1. Individuals and interactions over processes and tools  
2. Working software over comprehensive documentation  
3. Customer collaboration over contract negotiation  
4. Responding to change over following a plan

### The 12 Principles of Agile

1. Our highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.  
2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.  
3. Deliver working software frequently, from a couple of weeks to a couple of months.  
4. Business people and developers must work together daily throughout the project.  
5. Build projects around motivated individuals. Give them the environment and support they need.  
6. The most efficient and effective method of conveying information is face-to-face conversation.  
7. Working software is the primary measure of progress.  
8. Agile processes promote sustainable development. The sponsors, developers, and users should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely.  
9. Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility.  
10. Simplicity—the art of maximizing the amount of work not done—is essential.  
11. The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams.  
12. At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly.

## Module 2: Scrum 101 - A Deep Dive

### What is Scrum?

Scrum is the most popular framework for implementing Agile. It's a lightweight, iterative approach that helps teams deliver value quickly and adapt to change.

#### The 3 Pillars of Empiricism
- Transparency — Key aspects are visible to all.  
- Inspection — Regularly check progress.  
- Adaptation — Adjust to minimize deviation.

### The 5 Scrum Values
- Commitment  
- Courage  
- Focus  
- Openness  
- Respect

(Chart placeholder: donut showing equal values for the five items.)

### The 3 Scrum Roles (Accountabilities)

- The Product Owner (PO)  
  The "What" & "Why". Accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team.

- The Scrum Master (SM)  
  The "How (Process)". A servant-leader accountable for establishing Scrum as defined. Helps the team and organization adopt Scrum.

- The Developers  
  The "How (Execution)". A self-organizing, cross-functional group of professionals who do the work of delivering a Done Increment.

### The 3 Scrum Artifacts (Work & Value)

- Product Backlog  
  An emergent, ordered list of everything that is known to be needed in the product. It is the single source of work.

- Sprint Backlog  
  The plan for the Sprint composed of the Sprint Goal and selected Product Backlog items.

- Increment  
  A concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal; the sum of all Product Backlog items completed during a Sprint and previous Sprints.

## Module 3: Implementing Scrum - The 5 Events

Scrum is implemented through a series of 5 events that create a regular cadence, or "heartbeat," for the project.

### The Scrum Process Flow

- Product Backlog  
  ↓
- 1. Sprint Planning — Define Sprint Goal & select work  
  ↓
- Sprint Backlog — The plan for this Sprint  
  ↓
- 2. The Sprint (1–4 Weeks) — The container for all other events. Work is done here.  
  - 3. Daily Scrum (15 min) — Daily inspection and adaptation of the plan  
  ↓
- "Done" Increment  
  ↓
- 4. Sprint Review — Inspect the Increment with stakeholders. Adapt Product Backlog.  
- 5. Sprint Retrospective — Inspect the process. Plan improvements for next Sprint.  
⟲ Cycle Repeats

### Sprint Timeboxing (Based on 4-Week Sprint)

(Chart placeholder: pie chart showing hours in a 4-week sprint.)

Example distribution (hours in a 4-week sprint, 160h total):
- Development Work: 140h  
- Sprint Planning: 8h  
- Daily Scrums (Total): 5h  
- Sprint Review: 4h  
- Sprint Retrospective: 3h

### Event Details (high level)
- The Sprint: The "heartbeat" of Scrum, a fixed-length event of one month or less which contains all other events.  
- Sprint Planning: Lays out the work to be performed. Timeboxed to a maximum of 8 hours for a one-month Sprint.  
- Daily Scrum: A 15-minute event for the Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog.  
- Sprint Review: Inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations. Timeboxed to a maximum of 4 hours for a one-month Sprint.  
- Sprint Retrospective: Plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness. Timeboxed to a maximum of 3 hours for a one-month Sprint.

## Module 4: Applying Agile in the Organization

While Scrum is designed for a single team, larger organizations often need frameworks to scale Agile.

### Scaling Agile: SAFe

The Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) is one of the most common frameworks for applying Agile principles at an enterprise level. It provides a structured approach for large organizations to coordinate multiple Agile teams (called "Agile Release Trains") working on a single solution.

### The 4 Levels of SAFe
- Full SAFe — The most comprehensive level, integrating all others.  
- Portfolio SAFe — Provides strategic guidance and investment funding.  
- Large Solution SAFe — For complex solutions requiring multiple Agile Release Trains.  
- Essential SAFe — The most basic configuration and starting point.
